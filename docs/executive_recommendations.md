# Executive Recommendations

The final executive action plan converts the portfolio analysis into ten
decision areas.

## 1. Scale Winning Products Instead of Expanding the Entire Portfolio

**Evidence:** Volume Drivers generate the majority of customer
engagement and Signature Products outperform the broader portfolio.

**Recommendation:** Expand proven Volume Drivers and Signature Products
across additional restaurants and cities rather than continuously
expanding assortment.

**Expected direction:** Higher engagement, better marketing efficiency
and faster revenue growth.

## 2. Rationalize the Dormant Portfolio

**Evidence:** Dormant Core is the largest inactive segment, while
Dormant Premium contains expensive products with little engagement.

**Recommendation:** Review, redesign, reposition or remove
low-engagement products.

**Expected direction:** Lower complexity, stronger menu productivity and
more focused portfolios.

## 3. Adopt a Dual Portfolio Growth Strategy

Use two complementary growth engines:

-   Volume Drivers for reach and customer acquisition
-   Premium Winners for profitability and premium positioning

## 4. Replace National Standardization with City-Specific Strategies

City-level analysis shows meaningful differences in customer engagement
and premium adoption. Pricing, promotions, menu composition and premium
strategy should therefore be localized.

## 5. Use Hyderabad as the Primary Innovation & Launch Market

Hyderabad leads the displayed engagement and restaurant-efficiency
views. The recommendation is to use it as a pilot market for new menu
launches, premium offerings and promotional experiments before broader
rollout.

## 6. Expand Premium Selectively, Not Universally

Premium expansion should be concentrated in cities and categories where
demand and willingness to pay are already demonstrated.

## 7. Build Around High-Performing Local Restaurant Partners

High-performing local brands provide useful benchmarks for engagement,
ratings and operating practices. Partnership and replication
opportunities should be explored.

## 8. Optimize Menus for Focus Rather Than Size

A larger assortment is not treated as an objective. Focused menus with
proven demand can improve operational efficiency and customer choice.

## 9. Strengthen Customer Trust Through Consistent Service Quality

Top-performing restaurants consistently show strong dining and delivery
ratings. Service quality should therefore remain part of the
portfolio-management agenda.

## 10. Shift from Descriptive Reporting to Continuous Portfolio Management

The Product Strategy framework should be reused as an ongoing management
system: monitor, classify, reclassify and adjust investment as customer
engagement changes.

## Executive Principle

The project recommends disciplined portfolio management rather than
continuous menu expansion: **scale winners, selectively premiumize,
rationalize the dormant tail, localize market strategy, and continuously
re-evaluate product roles.**
